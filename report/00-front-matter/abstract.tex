\chapter*{Abstract}

Expression pedals let musicians change a sound parameter with the foot. Inside, they hold a passive potentiometer wired to the three contacts of a plug, but no standard defines this wiring, so a pedal on a mismatched input reads reversed, covers only part of its range or does not respond at all. This project presents an adapter with four jacks that identifies the network behind each jack electrically and sends the pedal position as a Musical Instrument Digital Interface control change over the Universal Serial Bus. Like an automatic component tester, it switches every contact to shared pull-up and pull-down resistors, reads the voltages with 24-bit analog-to-digital converters and solves the star-equivalent resistances, which tell potentiometer pedals of either wiring, footswitches and rheostats apart. Since this is slow, an identified pedal is then followed with a single reading per update. Every available pedal was identified within \qty{0.1}{\second} to \qty{0.3}{\second} and updated 95 to 188 times per second, with a position noise below \qty{4}{\percent} of one output step. The results show that a pedal of unknown wiring can be identified electrically and followed fast and precisely enough for musical control.
