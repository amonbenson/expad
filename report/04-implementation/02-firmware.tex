\section{Firmware}
\label{sec:firmware}

The firmware is written in Rust \cite{rust2026} on the asynchronous Embassy framework \cite{embassy2026}, so measurement, USB and WiFi run as concurrent tasks on the RP2350 of the Pico~2~W \cite{rpi2026rp2350, benson2026expad}. The solver and the per-jack state machine form a library without hardware dependency. The solver was first explored in an interactive simulator \cite{benson2026topologysim}, and on the host computer, unit tests drive the library against a simulated star network.

One \emph{jack monitor} per jack runs the stages of \cref{fig:firmware-overview}: rail measurement and plug detection, identification by solves, and following, from which it returns to identification when the pedal no longer matches or to plug detection when the plug is pulled. The monitor never touches the hardware: it hands out one reading at a time, given as the drives and the contact to read, and takes the voltage back. A scheduler can thus interleave the jacks, and both ADCs convert in parallel, each for its most overdue jack. \Cref{fig:jack-monitor-states} shows the full state diagram, explained in \cref{sec:appendix-firmware}.

The target of 50 to 100 updates per second and jack was set in the discussion after milestone~1. At 100 updates per second, the interval is \qty{10}{\milli\second}, the bound commonly given for the latency between a gesture and its audible result \cite{wessel2002problems}. The first firmware solved every jack in turn and reached 2.6 updates per second: each of the nine readings per jack changed the channel of the ADC, and the sinc\textsuperscript{3} filter of the AD7718 needs three conversion periods to settle after a change \cite{adi2001ad7718}, \qty{9.5}{\milli\second} at \qty{315}{\hertz}, and with a constant overhead of about \qty{0.7}{\milli\second}, one reading took \qty{10.2}{\milli\second}. Two steps fixed this. First, readings were made faster: a characterization of every filter rate (\cref{tab:adc-characterization}) selected \qty{819}{\hertz}, where one reading including the channel change takes \qty{4.36}{\milli\second}, 2.3 times faster than at \qty{315}{\hertz}. Its noise of \qty{0.39}{\milli\volt} is rounded up to $\sigma_V = \qty{0.5}{\milli\volt}$ for the tolerances. The noisier readings made the poor conditioning of a wiper on the tip visible: the standard deviation of the solved position rose from \num{2588} to \num{4880} parts per million (ppm) of the travel, so the minimum conditioning was raised from 0.05 to 0.5. Second, fewer readings are taken: an identified pedal is followed with one reading, and an empty jack costs one plug check every \qty{50}{\milli\second}.

The position is stretched from the range set for the jack to $u \in [0, 1]$, inverted if set, and bent by the drive curve $y = (1+b)\,u/(1 - b + 2bu)$ with $b = 0.9\,d$ for a setting $d \in [-1, 1]$. This is Schlick's bias function \cite{schlick1994bias}, which keeps both ends fixed and gives the inverse curve for $-d$, so it approximately straightens pedals with logarithmic or antilogarithmic potentiometers alike. The adapter is a class-compliant USB MIDI device, which hosts use without a dedicated driver \cite{usbif1999midi}. It sends 7-bit control changes, on controller 11 (expression) or 1 (modulation) by default \cite{midi2026controlchange}, once a value differs from the last one by more than 0.6 steps.

During development, the adapter froze on a macOS host, a problem also reported for other embassy-rp devices \cite{embassy2023issue1663}, likely because the USB driver of embassy-rp 0.10 keeps a stale suspend latched across the bus reset and then ignores the host \cite{embassy2026pr6693}. The firmware therefore wraps the driver, clears the suspend and ends transfers once the host disconnects instead of waiting forever \cite{embassy2024issue3426}. A WiFi access point with a captive portal, which phones and tablets open as the sign-in page of the network, serves the web interface. It shows the network, arms and contact voltages of each jack and holds its settings, such as the MIDI controller, range and drive curve.
