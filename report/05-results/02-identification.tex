\section{Identification and Position}
\label{sec:results-identification}

\scaffold{
  \textbf{Paragraph 1, pedals} (about 120 words, \cref{tab:pedals}). Every pedal at hand was identified as its type and wiring. The M-Audio EX-P carries 8 to \qty{15}{\percent} of its total in its wiper lead; with the original wiper limit of \qty{10}{\percent} it was classified as \enquote{other} over most of its travel, which led to the \qty{25}{\percent} limit and the near-end class. No two-wire rheostat pedal was available; a bare \qty{100}{\kilo\ohm} potentiometer between tip and sleeve stood in for one. Several sustain pedals behaved like the Boss FS-5U. No plug-cycle test was made; occasional misdetections occurred, most often a footswitch taken for a rheostat when plugged in slowly or when the jack was turned during operation, and the firmware recovered on its own within a few seconds.
}

\scaffold{
  \textbf{Paragraph 2, accuracy} (about 70 words). A sweep of precision resistors, planned for accuracy and linearity, was not made for lack of time. The ohmmeter comparison of \cref{tab:pedals} stands in: the EX-P track agrees within \qty{0.1}{\percent}; the EV-30 was solved \qty{11}{\percent} high at bring-up (at \qty{315}{\hertz}) and at about \qty{9.4}{\kilo\ohm} from the voltage levels of an oscilloscope capture; the EV-30's minimum knob, set differently between these measurements, is a possible explanation. The total only sets the settling time and the rheostat scale; the position depends on voltage ratios alone.
}

\scaffold{
  \textbf{Paragraph 3, position noise} (about 40 words). With the EV-30 held still, solved positions scatter by \num{274} parts per million of travel and single wiper readings by \num{203} parts per million (\cref{tab:adc-characterization}); one MIDI step is \num{7874} parts per million, so the jitter stays below 1/29 of a step and the sent value does not change.
}
