\section{Identification and Position}
\label{sec:results-identification}

With a working signal chain, every pedal at hand was identified as its type and wiring (\cref{tab:pedals}). The M-Audio EX-P carries 8 to \qty{15}{\percent} of its total resistance in its wiper lead; with the original wiper limit of \qty{10}{\percent}, it was classified as other over most of its travel, which led to the limit of \qty{25}{\percent} and the near-end class. A bare \qty{100}{\kilo\ohm} potentiometer between tip and sleeve stood in for a rheostat pedal, which was not available, and several sustain pedals behaved like the Boss FS-5U. No systematic plug-cycle test was made. Occasional misdetections occurred, most often a footswitch taken for a rheostat when it was plugged in slowly or turned in the jack, and the firmware recovered on its own within a few seconds.

The detected total resistances of multiple pedals were compared with an ohmmeter reading in \cref{tab:pedals}. The track of the EX-P agrees within \qty{0.1}{\percent}. The EV-30 was solved \qty{11}{\percent} high at bring-up, still at \qty{315}{\hertz}, and at about \qty{9.4}{\kilo\ohm} from the voltage levels of an oscilloscope capture; its minimum knob, set differently between these measurements, is a possible explanation. The total resistance only sets the settling time and the scale of a rheostat, whereas the position depends on voltage ratios alone.

With the EV-30 held still, solved positions scattered by \num{274} parts per million (ppm) of the travel and single wiper readings by \num{203}~ppm (\cref{tab:adc-characterization}). One MIDI step is \num{7874}~ppm, so the jitter stays at or below about 1/29 of a step, and the sent value does not change.
