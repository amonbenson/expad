\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Expression pedals are foot controllers that let a musician change sound parameters while both hands stay on their instrument \cite{mission2014expression}. Inside, an expression pedal is fully passive: a potentiometer of typically \qtyrange{5}{50}{\kilo\ohm} connects to a \qty{6.35}{\milli\metre} ``TRS'' jack, exposing three \emph{contacts} (the tip, the ring, and the sleeve). The connected device applies a voltage across the track and reads the wiper back \cite{mission2014expression, nonlinearlabs2026pedals}.

No standard defines how the potentiometer is wired to these contacts \cite{mission2014expression}: Boss and Roland pedals carry the wiper on the tip, Yamaha pedals on the ring \cite{nonlinearlabs2026pedals}, and some manufacturers connect only tip and sleeve while acting as a variable resistor, a \emph{rheostat}. On an input that expects a different wiring, a pedal therefore reads reversed, covers only part of its range or does not respond at all. Pedals with a polarity switch can mitigate this, but the musician has to know the required setting \cite{maudio2026exp}.

Concretely, this project builds an adapter with four jacks that identifies the network behind each jack electrically, follows the pedal, and sends its position as a Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) control change over the Universal Serial Bus (USB) \cite{benson2026proposal}. It covers potentiometer pedals of either wiring, sustain switches, and rheostats. The identification applies the principle of automatic component testers, which drive the pins of an unknown part through known resistors and read the resulting voltages with an analog-to-digital converter (ADC) \cite{frejek2011avr}. The solver was first explored in an interactive simulator \cite{benson2026topologysim}, tested on a breadboard, and runs on the device as a library that can be tested separately on a host computer \cite{benson2026expad}.
