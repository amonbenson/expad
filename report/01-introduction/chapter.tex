\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Expression pedals are foot controllers that act like a remote knob and let a musician change a sound parameter, such as the volume or the feedback of a delay \cite{mission2014expression}, while both hands stay on the instrument. Inside, an expression pedal is fully passive: a potentiometer of typically \qtyrange{5}{50}{\kilo\ohm} is wired to a \qty{6.35}{\milli\metre} plug with three \emph{contacts}, the tip, the ring and the sleeve (TRS). The connected device applies a voltage across the track and reads the wiper back \cite{mission2014expression, nonlinearlabs2026pedals}.

No standard defines how the potentiometer is wired to these contacts \cite{mission2014expression}: Boss and Roland pedals carry the wiper on the tip, Yamaha pedals on the ring \cite{nonlinearlabs2026pedals}, and some pedals connect only tip and sleeve, acting as a variable resistor, a \emph{rheostat}. Sustain pedals are simple footswitches on the same kind of plug. On an input that expects a different wiring, a pedal therefore reads reversed, covers only part of its range or does not respond at all.

This project \cite{benson2026proposal} therefore builds an adapter with four jacks that identifies the network behind each jack electrically and sends the position of the pedal as a Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) control change over the Universal Serial Bus (USB). A control change carries a controller number and a 7-bit value from 0 to 127, which the music software assigns to a parameter \cite{midi2026controlchange}. The adapter covers potentiometer pedals of either wiring, footswitches and rheostats. One LED per jack shows its state, and a web interface over WiFi shows the identified networks and holds the settings. Like an automatic component tester, the adapter drives the contacts through known resistors and reads the resulting voltages with an analog-to-digital converter (ADC) \cite{frejek2011avr}. Since an identification needs several slow, high-resolution readings, the adapter identifies a pedal once when it is plugged in and then follows its position with a single reading per update.
