\section{Plug Detection and Following}
\label{sec:following}

A solve cannot tell an empty jack from a plugged cable with nothing behind it. The jacks therefore have a \emph{tip switch}, a fourth contact that touches the tip until a plug lifts it off \cite{neutrik2026nmj6hfd2}. The \emph{plug check} drives the tip low and the tip switch high. Without a plug, the two pull resistors divide the rail to \qty{1.25}{\volt}. With a plug, the tip is lifted off and the tip switch reads its full rail of \qty{2.5}{\volt}. A reading above three quarters of the span counts as a plug. Since only the tip is driven, no current flows through the pedal, so the check works for any wiring and position.

An identified potentiometer is followed with one reading per update: the track ends are driven, and only the wiper is read. The wiper carries no current, so its contact resistance does not enter the position
\begin{equation}
  p = \frac{V_\mathrm{w} - V_\mathrm{start}}{V_\mathrm{end} - V_\mathrm{start}}, \qquad 0 \leq p \leq 1,
  \label{eq:position}
\end{equation}
where $V_\mathrm{w}$ is the wiper reading and $V_\mathrm{start}$ and $V_\mathrm{end}$ are the readings of the track ends, driven low and high. The track starts at the sleeve whenever the sleeve is a track end, so the position rises away from the sleeve in both common wirings. One track end is read again every \qty{25}{\milli\second}. If the drop across its pull resistor changes, or the wiper leaves the span of the ends, the monitor loses track of the pedal and identifies it again.

Footswitches and rheostats are followed through the tip, with the tip high and the sleeve low. A resistance of at most \qty{0.2}{\kilo\ohm} reads as a closed footswitch, and three consecutive readings between open and closed mark a rheostat. While a jack is followed, the plug check runs every \qty{50}{\milli\second}.
