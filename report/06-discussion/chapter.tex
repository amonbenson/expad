\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

The results show that an unknown passive pedal can be identified in place with switchable pull resistors and a slow high-resolution ADC, and then followed with one conversion per update: every available pedal type and wiring was recognized within \qty{0.1}{\second} (\qty{0.3}{\second} with a rail measurement) and updated about 188 times per second, well above the target of 50 to \qty{100}{\hertz}, with a position noise far below one MIDI step. The claim is limited to the star equivalent: the adapter recognizes pedal classes and wirings, not arbitrary circuits, with thresholds set on a few pedals.

The update rate came from changing what is measured, not from faster hardware: the same ADC delivered 2.6 updates per second at first. Tolerances derived from the noise of a reading let one solver handle pedals of low and high resistance, and the host tests made each solver defect reproducible within seconds. The subtler defects, however, surfaced in noise and repeatability observations rather than in functional tests: a poorly conditioned solve, a wiper limit too narrow for a resistive wiper lead, and misdetections of moving plugs.

Accuracy and linearity were only checked against an ohmmeter, where one pedal's total deviated by \qty{11}{\percent}, possibly because of its minimum knob; a sweep with precision resistors would settle this. Misdetections of a moving plug are corrected only after a few seconds, possibly because a slowly inserted footswitch passes through intermediate resistances that the follower takes for a rheostat; a second solve shortly after identification would catch them earlier. The rail measurement adds about \qty{214}{\milli\second} to the first plug-in after \qty{30}{\second} and to every unplug, because it runs in the foreground. Two pedals on one ADC halve each other's rate, since each ADC converts one contact at a time. Potentiometer pedals need a stereo cable, a property of the pedal that no interface can remove. The settings are lost at power-off, as they are not yet written to flash memory, and the 7-bit MIDI value discards most of the measured resolution. Finally, WiFi connections to one Android tablet still drop occasionally despite the captive portal; their cause is open.
