\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

The results show that an unknown passive pedal can be identified in place with switchable pull resistors and a slow high-resolution ADC, and then followed with one reading per update: every available pedal type and wiring was identified within \qty{0.1}{\second} (\qty{0.3}{\second} with a rail measurement) and updated 95 to 188 times per second, above the target of 50 to 100, with a position noise far below one MIDI step. The claim is limited to the star equivalent: the adapter identifies pedal classes and wirings, not arbitrary circuits, with thresholds set on a few pedals.

The update rate rose 70-fold through changes to the firmware alone. Tolerances derived from the noise of a reading let one solver handle pedals of low and high resistance, and the host tests made each solver defect reproducible within seconds. However, subtle defects surfaced in noise and repeatability observations rather than in functional tests: a poorly conditioned solve, a wiper limit too narrow for a resistive wiper lead, and misdetections of moving plugs.

The evaluation leaves gaps. Accuracy was only checked against an ohmmeter, where the total of the EV-30 deviated by \qty{11}{\percent}, possibly because of its minimum knob, and linearity and the settling model at high resistance were not measured. A moving plug is occasionally misdetected for a few seconds, possibly because a slowly inserted footswitch passes through intermediate resistances that the follower takes for a rheostat. A second solve shortly after identification would catch this earlier, but would lengthen the identification time.

The design has limits as well. The rail measurement runs in the foreground and adds about \qty{214}{\milli\second} to a plug-in or unplug once the rails are older than \qty{30}{\second}, which is usually the case for an unplug. Two pedals on one ADC halve each other's rate, since each ADC converts one contact at a time, and raise the worst-case latency above \qty{10}{\milli\second}. With \qty{1}{\kilo\ohm} pull resistors, a \qty{500}{\kilo\ohm} pedal drops only \qty{5}{\milli\volt} across a pull resistor, little margin for the current checks. The settings can only be changed through the web interface and are lost at power-off, the 7-bit MIDI value discards most of the measured resolution, and the supply accepts only center-negative plugs. WiFi connections to one Android tablet still drop occasionally despite the captive portal, for reasons not yet known.
