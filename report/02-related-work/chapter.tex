\scaffold{
  Budget: about 180 words in two paragraphs without subsections (writing guide, section 3.4: group names in italics at their definition, each paragraph closes by placing the project against the groups).

  \textbf{Paragraph 1, pedals and interfaces.} \emph{Switchable pedals} carry a selector that swaps tip and ring, such as the M-Audio EX-P \cite{maudio2026exp}; \emph{configurable inputs} offer presets per pedal, as the Nonlinear Labs C15 \cite{nonlinearlabs2026pedals}; \emph{fixed-wiring interfaces}, mostly do-it-yourself projects, read one wiring and send USB MIDI \cite{gaspar2026diyexpression}. \emph{Pedal-sensing interfaces} such as the Audiofront MIDI Expression \cite{audiofront2026midiexpression} and the DOREMiDi MPC-10 \cite{doremidi2026mpc10} detect pedal types and polarities but document neither their method nor which networks they distinguish. Closing: this project measures the network itself, so its classification and its limits are documented and testable.

  \textbf{Paragraph 2, component identification.} The \emph{AVR transistor tester} identifies type and pinout of a part on three test pins, each connected to port pins through a \qty{680}{\ohm} and a \qty{470}{\kilo\ohm} resistor and read by the ADC \cite{frejek2011avr, kuebbeler2023transistortester}. The underlying problem is the discrete inverse problem for resistor networks \cite{curtis1990determining}. Closing: this project applies the principle to a pedal at run time and adds a mode that follows an identified pedal with a single conversion.
}
